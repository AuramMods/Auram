# Auram server performance, November 24, 2026

## Priorities

1. **Patch Adapt's material-value recipe lookup.** It repeatedly scans the full recipe registry while resolving newly discovered items and blocks. This is the strongest patch candidate for the multi-second stalls.
2. **Attribute datapack command time by function ID, then patch the hottest loops.** Selectors and entity NBT serialization consume time in every minute of the capture. The shipped `ggunz` loop is the strongest candidate for the per-tick cost. Incendium is a candidate for the scheduled work.
3. **Optimize Tom's Simple Storage at the terminal update and sync boundaries.** Both paths rose sharply in the same minute as the 3.92 s tick. Start with redundant comparisons and copies when terminal contents are unchanged.
4. **Benchmark AuramCore's structure-start terrain sampler setup.** This is the largest observed fresh-chunk cost. The profiler points to building the sampling graph, especially for Lithostitched jigsaws, fossils, and Nyctophobia mushrooms.
5. **Take Astral Sorcery's attribute lookup as a small direct patch.** Replace the repeated registry stream with a lifecycle-scoped lookup and preserve the first registered match.

## High-upside opportunities

### Opportunity 1 — Adapt recipe index

- **Evidence.** `ModMaterialValuePort.recipesFor` accounts for **20.78 sampled s** on the main thread, including **14.01 s** from looking at new blocks and **6.70 s** from item pickup. In the installed 2.2.7 jar, `MaterialValue.getValue` and `resolve` allocate per-resolution maps and walk the dependency graph on a miss (lines 207–258). `ModMaterialValuePort.recipesFor` iterates `ModRecipeHost.ordered` for each uncached material in that walk (lines 34–52). `convert` extracts the result to discover whether the recipe matches (lines 65–95). Several seconds in the spike windows are under this path.
- **Patch.** In Adapt, build an ordered output-item → recipes index from the same supported shaped, shapeless, and single-item recipes. Build it once when the recipe set settles, and invalidate it on server stop, recipe replacement and datapack reload. Keep the current namespace exclusion, item identity, recipe order, ingredient choice and recursive cycle handling. Index raw recipes first so tag-sensitive ingredient expansion still happens at the existing boundary. A separate bounded warm-up or converted-recipe cache is only warranted if resolving the smaller result lists still stalls. AuramCore can wrap `recipesFor` as a version-guarded compatibility patch if deploying Adapt directly is impractical.
- **Expected gain high · Risk low–medium · Complexity low–medium · Confidence high.** The pack repeatedly pays a whole-registry scan to find a small result set. Validate per-item recipe IDs and order, exact material values and discovery XP against stock. Exercise first sight and first pickup after startup and `/reload`, recipes added or removed by scripts, recursive recipes, and a burst of distinct items with a storage menu open.

### Opportunity 2 — Datapack selector and NBT hot loops

- **Evidence.** `ServerFunctionManager.execute` accounts for **30.00 s**. **22.71 s** came from the regular tick tag and **7.28 s** from scheduled functions. Entity selector paths account for **23.34 s**, including **13.51 s** under traversal of the loaded entity lists and **7.87 s** under selector-driven `Entity.saveWithoutId`. Most of that serialization occurs in player selectors. These figures overlap the function totals. Installed `guns1-20-0.1.2-dp.zip` registers `ggunz:loop` in the tick tag. Its loop has global selectors on lines 2, 29 and 35–44, a nearby-item pass on line 30, and repeated selected-item NBT checks on lines 32 and 47–52. Incendium 5.3.5's `clocks/main.mcfunction` reschedules itself every tick and scans for tagged entities on lines 3–28. Also inspect the registered Scorched, Dungeons Plus and Reworked Phantoms tick functions if they appear in the timing trace.
- **Patch.** Put a lightweight AuramCore timing probe around function execution and command entries that exceed a threshold. Track the top-level function ID, nested ID where available, and command index before attributing the totals to a pack. Start with shipped datapack overrides. Replace confirmed equipment checks with exact equipment predicates or a Java check of the held stack. Consolidate repeated identical lookups within a function only where the intervening commands preserve their result. Narrow entity queries by known type, dimension or indexed tags after comparing target sets. A scoped vanilla mixin for simple `SelectedItem` patterns is a fallback if several packs need the same fix. Generic NBT selectors, Forge capability serialization and arbitrary cross-command caches have a much larger equivalence surface.
- **Expected gain high for steady tick headroom · Risk low for verified local rewrites, medium for generic selector hooks · Complexity low–medium · Confidence high in the cost, medium in the namespace attribution.** Replay idle, exploration, held-item changes, active projectiles, and Incendium scripted encounters. Compare matched entities, scoreboard state, emitted effects and p95/p99 MSPT.

### Opportunity 3 — AuramCore terrain-probe construction and structure starts

- **Evidence.** The grouped Minecraft `Worker-Main` stacks had **73.05 s** in `ChunkGenerator.createStructures`. Lithostitched `AlternateJigsawGenerator.generate` contributed **21.66 s**, `NetherFossilStructure.findGenerationPoint` **18.77 s**, and Nyctophobia `MushroomStructure.findGenerationPoint` **10.72 s**. These are caller totals. **49.94 s** under `TerrainCell.<init>` includes **45.22 s** under `NoiseChunk.<init>` and **10.49 s** in hash/equality calls inside terrain-cell construction. Only **3.38 s** was visible in ordinary `NoiseChunk.fillSlice` under the terrain-cell constructor. Another **17.52 s** went through vanilla `iterateNoiseColumn`, including Biomes We've Gone arches, mushroom starts, Embers caves, Astral templates and Malum wells. The AuramCore 1.0.43 source at `63b401e` shows where those costs originate in `TerrainCell.java` lines 54–76, `TerrainBatch.java` lines 65–96, and `NoiseChunkMappingMixin.java` lines 36–69. A wrapper's inclusive time also includes the vanilla work it invokes.
- **Patch.** First benchmark existing terrain batching against the vanilla call at each consumer and count `TerrainCell` opens, cache hits and bounded-to-full replacements. Then prototype a per-`NoiseChunk` identity front cache for explicitly supported immutable density nodes, using the existing structural cache for first encounters and distinct equal nodes. Alternatively share an immutable mapping plan across samplers and instantiate all interpolation, aquifer, blending and cell-local state for each sampler. Keep the existing scope and fallback for unfamiliar density graphs. Extend existing terrain reuse to remaining vanilla height/column callers only where measured coordinates overlap or batching reduces work. Avoid increasing cache lifetime without a reuse measurement.
- **Expected gain high for cold-chunk throughput · Risk medium · Complexity medium · Confidence high for the hotspot, medium for the proposed optimization.** Use the existing four-CPU AuramCore cold-world harness with fresh identical saves. Measure structure CPU per task, chunk-ready p95, fresh FULL/s and tick p99 during concurrent exploration. Compare complete structure starts and pieces, biome palettes, blocks and fluid ticks across multiple seeds, vertical ranges and blended boundaries. Run the unchanged build twice first to measure the pack's existing worldgen variability.

## Operational checks and watchlist

- **G1 GC and allocation pressure.** The JVM snapshot shows 239 young collections averaging 56.9 ms, zero reported old collections, and around 9.70 GB heap used of 12.58 GB committed. The main thread has only 0.23 s and 0.35 s under `GC_active` in the 3.92 s and 4.18 s windows respectively. GC could explain some shorter spikes and still deserves a timestamped GC log or JFR. Patch high-allocation recipe, command and worldgen paths first. Use fixed heap and flags in the A/B, then test one JVM variable at a time if pauses still correlate.
- **Chunk simulation budget.** 3,026–4,643 loaded chunks with two players is enough to measure the effect of reducing simulation distance from the live value of 10 to 8 on a test copy. This changes how far mobs and machines tick, so treat it as an operational tradeoff and leave it out of the behavior-preserving patch series. Compare force-loaded farms, loaded counts, mob behavior and exploration p99 with the same view distance.
- **Distant Horizons and scheduling.** Worldgen queue and progress threads are dominated by queue wait and sleep. Vanilla workers were active during fresh generation. A quiet DH queue can still submit work that appears under vanilla workers, so trace ticket origin and queues before adjusting threads. If near-player chunk work is competing with optional LOD generation, check the live health throttle and the existing AuramCore `WorldGenerationPriorityMixin`. A/B LOD generation with tick metrics, near-player FULL throughput and LOD completion. Keep the cloud of parked worker weights out of the CPU budget.
- **Older freeze suspects.** The strongest wait in the earlier Nether-return investigation was a synchronous support-block lookup after movement or a dimension change. This capture had **0.032 s** through `getChunk → managedBlock`, **1.29 s** in `processUnloads`, and **2.44 s** across selected save/serializer stacks. Midnight and Aether paths were small, and the sampled Relics fluid path was **2.14 s** without a large chunk-wait stack. This run provides no reason to reopen the old load-stall fixes first. If the original Nether-to-home freeze returns, capture repeated exact transitions with the existing AuramCore chunk-wait trace before changing unloading or save behavior.
- **Entity/spawn workload.** Zebroid ticks accounted for **11.86 s**, schooling fish **9.25 s**, `NaturalSpawner.spawnForChunk` **11.44 s**, and goal tick paths **12.51 s**. Those totals overlap and include ordinary mob work. A larger entity fixture can identify specific redundant goals, collision queries or repeated registry checks. Broad tick skipping may change AI and farm behavior and has weak support here.
- **Secondary tools and UI.** Netty compression accounted for **6.06 s** on its workers, whereas main-thread chunk packet construction accounted for **0.15 s**. The main `Connection.tick` spikes mostly reflected gameplay such as Adapt item pickup. The named Auram vapor, wind, RF, and surface-map paths contributed **0.40 s** on the main thread, KubeJS paths about **0.28 s**, and ordinary recipe-manager lookups about **0.07 s**. These paths are lower priority in this workload. Reassess compression only if a larger player test shows CPU or bandwidth contention.
- **Unattributed spikes.** The 1.06 s and 668 ms windows need timestamps or a slow-tick-only trace to pin to individual events. The save and GC samples in the capture cannot identify a specific pause. Keep the 200–280 ms spikes in the validation workload as well as the longest stalls.
